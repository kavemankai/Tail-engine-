# 01 · Telltale technical deconstruction

**Scope.** The Wolf Among Us, Batman: The Telltale Series, The Walking Dead and Tales from the Borderlands. Audit date 16 November 2026 (Adelaide). See 02 for revisions and 04 for encounter evidence.

| Evidence level | Finding | Limit |
|---|---|---|

## What is in a .chore

`parse_chore()` builds a MetaStream reader and dispatches into `decode_chore()`. The reader tracks header type/version identifiers and block boundaries. This lets parsers handle layout variants and skip bounded unknown data. Later handles resolve resource names through object-info structures, whereas the decoded older form can store names as strings. Explorer's `extract_handles()` walks typed fields and can supplement them with a raw name scan. Treat the latter as a lead, with lower confidence than a typed field.

| Question | Pinned evidence and scope | Equivalent in the proposed Godot system |
|---|---|---|
| Scene objects and roles | Top-level scene name/dependencies, plus `ChoreAgent` names, integer resource indices, properties, position, orientation and attachment. Later models also expose actor/agent binding fields. A scene defines the actual agents. | Encounter-local role IDs bound to scene nodes by the host. Scene-owned entry/exit markers and prop sockets. |
| Animation | `ChoreResource` has handle/object info, resource group, length, priority, embedded/control animation and resource property fields. Explorer skips parts of embedded control animation. `.anm` and `.skl` have their own formats. | `AnimationLibrary` and an authored `Animation` with indexed role bindings. Same kit skeleton for authored and imported clips. |
| Time-based scheduling | Chore length/flags, resource block start/end, looping and scale, with typed keyed properties among decoded leaves. `ChoreResource.Block` in ToolKit corroborates the schedule fields. | Animation tracks, a small set of named method markers and local time coordinates. |
| Cameras | Camera assets/properties and handles are supported evidence. ToolKit has `CameraSelect.CameraNames` and Chore camera-related fields such as `ResetNavCamsOnExit`. These do not establish a universal shot track schema or a specific retail camera-stack implementation. | Shot markers, explicit cut or blend policy, a scene camera adapter and lease/restore. |
| Sound, music and localisation | Handle/property evidence and localisation/dependency fields establish ties to audio and dialogue. Explorer also documents separate phoneme timing assets. The mod shows how it starts voice and acting together. Music state machines, mix precedence and audio clock ownership remain unresolved. | Audio tracks/cues for local contact and voice, separate music bus, named line/cue references. Synchronise gestures and SFX to animation markers. |
| Nesting | Handle types and name references can express dependencies on other authored assets, with `AnimOrChore` elsewhere in ToolKit. A reference alone does not establish general recursive playback, conditional subgraphs or authoring support in the four episodes. | Reusable leaf beats first. Add reusable sub-sequences when two concrete encounters need them. |
| Conditions | Property sets can carry keyed values and the decoder handles rule-like leaves. ToolKit declares `ChoreAgent.AgentEnabledRule` and comments it as Bone-only. These are productive leads for activation/selection, with version scope. | Typed flags or prop conditions owned by the director. |
| Input, failure and branch edges | This audit found no decoded general QTE, input-window or success/failure graph in `.chore`. ToolKit's separate Dlg layer includes `DlgConditionInput` (base marker, no button definition), `DlgConditionTime` (duration and seconds), `DlgNodeConditional`, `DlgNodeScript`, `DlgNodeChore` and links. A mod chooses the next clip in Lua. Retail game-specific ownership remains unresolved. | Gameplay input gate evaluates actions. Directed outcome IDs select beats, and the game owns persistent consequences. |

Explorer documents 1,929/1,929 parsed early chores. That is an author-reported corpus result. Its documentation also identifies opaque fields and drift, including older empty/default records and unresolved values. Compare its 12-field early reconstruction with ToolKit's version-dependent field superset before generalising anything to Batman or later builds.

## Reconstruct the separation, then author the experience

1. **Scene context** binds player, opponent, prop and cameras. Chore evidence supports durable scene/role/resource references.
2. **A beat** lays out anticipation, movement, acting, shot changes and sound. Each branch begins in a compatible state and schedules one or more authored audiovisual responses.
3. **Interaction** evaluates an explicit gate, expiry and context choice. The mod and the Dlg types demonstrate workable separation patterns. The location of the retail QTE evaluator is open.
4. **Narrative** stores the durable consequence and opens the next cue. Gameplay sources distinguish tactical execution, local presentation and character decisions. Saved flag IDs, scene-load protocol and retail retry checkpoints were outside the evidence obtained.
5. **Handback** restores control, camera and dialogue exactly once. This is the Godot integration contract.

Sources for character-centred pacing and the difference between action and relationship decisions: [Saving Doug, GDC 2013](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1019053/Saving-Doug-Empathy-Character-and), [Telltale co-founder interview](https://gameinformer.com/2019/04/14/telltale-co-founder-opens-up-about-studios-troubling-closure), [The Walking Dead production postmortem](https://media.gdcvault.com/GD_Mag_Archives/GDM_March_2013.pdf).
